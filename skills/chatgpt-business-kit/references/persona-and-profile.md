# Identity verification (Persona) and the profile

## What Persona unlocks
Claim approval alone gives you nothing editable. After Persona approval you get: the blue check, publishing profile changes, connecting a product feed, Insights, and creating an agent.

## Persona prep list (do this before you click Verify)
- [ ] The person verifying is the owner or someone authorised to represent the business (it's **their** government ID; an agency can't do it for the client).
- [ ] Government-issued photo ID, valid and not expired.
- [ ] Business registration details (registration / incorporation number, tax or EIN where relevant).
- [ ] Proof you're authorised to represent the business (e.g. you're named in the registration, or a letter/role document).
- [ ] **Name, address and registration/tax number entered exactly as on official records.** Compare against: registration certificate, tax documents, the website footer/legal page, Google Business Profile. Note every difference (Ltd vs Limited, suite numbers, old address, trading name vs legal name).
- [ ] Sole proprietors: decide upfront whether you're entering your legal name or trading name, and match the document you'll upload. Launch reports [anecdotal] describe failures with no explanation after trying name variations.
- [ ] **Profile ready first** (description, logo, policies): if Persona creates a new profile, approval **publishes it automatically**, and a published profile can't be deleted, unpublished or hidden.

If it fails: follow the email, use "Try again" if offered, fix the mismatch you most likely hit, and keep a note of what you entered each time.

## Profile fields
Editable after both approvals: website, business description, product or service category, logo, delivery policy, return policy, cover photo and other optional details. OpenAI's policy also covers hours, contact details, address or service area, products/services. Every edit is reviewed by OpenAI before it appears; ChatGPT may still use other sources. Character limits aren't published.

## Profile template (fill from the website, not from memory)
| Field | How to write it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Category | The words a customer would type, not your internal jargon | "Video editing app", not "AI-native creative infrastructure" |
| Description | One sentence a customer would say to a friend: **what you sell + who it's for** (+ one proof point if true) | "Edit your videos right inside WhatsApp." |
| Website | The canonical homepage (https, no tracking params) | |
| Delivery policy | Where you ship, how long, cost or "free over X", in one or two lines, **matching the site word for word on numbers** | "Ships across the US in 3–5 business days. Free over $50." |
| Return policy | Window, condition, who pays return shipping, sale-item rule | "30-day returns on unused items. Sale items: store credit only." |
| Logo / cover | Square logo you own the rights to; a cover that shows the product or place | |
| Hours / contact / area | Same as Google Business Profile and the website | |

Rules from OpenAI's policy: no fake or duplicate profiles, no fabricated reviews or endorsements, no unsupported guarantees ("#1", "best in town" without proof), no hidden fees or conditions, only images you have rights to, honest links.

## Consistency check (the most useful 10 minutes)
Make a table: fact · profile says · website says · Google/Bing/Yelp say · fix. Check price range, shipping cost/time, return window, hours, address/service area, phone, email, product names. Any mismatch means ChatGPT gets two answers; fix the website first, then the profile.
